## Audience
- Who: people who save things from many apps (TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, articles, chats) and lose them across bookmarks, screenshots, saved tabs and notes. Curious learners, creators, founders, planners (trips, gifts, recipes).
- Search intent and phrases (inferred, not measured): "app to save links", "save ideas app", "read later app", "pocket alternative", "bookmark manager", "save tiktok instagram youtube links", "idea inbox app".
- Pains (their words):
  1. "bookmarks on Chrome, saved reels on Instagram, liked tweets on X, screenshots of TikToks, half-finished Notes." (hunted.space/product/saverything, maker voice)
  2. "Nothing was in one place, and nothing ever came back to me when I actually needed it." (same)
  3. "The problem, I find, with the app is that there is too much friction in capturing an idea." (mymind App Store review)
  4. "when I shared an instagram reel it added it as a link." (mymind review: shares saved as bare links)
  5. "AMAZING productivity tool but needs an offline mode desperately" (Notion review); "The only drawback is that it doesn't have offline support." (Raindrop review)
  6. Notes apps become "the junk drawer of your phone" (sashadas.substack.com/p/you-need-an-idea-inbox)
- Desired outcomes: one place for everything saved; save from any app in one tap; find it again instantly (search inside the content); works offline; calm, beautiful space.

## Category conventions
- Respect: verb-first 2 to 6 word headline over a cropped phone; screenshot 1 leads with the save-from-anywhere / share-sheet mechanism; show source-platform icons; dark mode screen.
- Break: everyone sells "saving". Nobody sells *getting it back*: search inside videos and articles, offline. And nobody uses a character; the category is cold and generic (mymind serif, Bookmarks orange). A friendly Wall mascot plus dark premium palette stands out.
